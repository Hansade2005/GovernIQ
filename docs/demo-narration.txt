This is GovernIQ. The regional evidence base for the North West Regional Assembly. It is not a proposal. Everything you are about to see is running now.

Since twenty seventeen, the crisis has broken the region's reporting chain. Field agents cannot move freely. Divisions file three to nine months late. Six or more sub-divisions cannot be reached at all. The Assembly decides on evidence that arrives long after the decision.

This is the Chamber view. Budget execution, active programmes, and the seven divisions, on one page. Every figure here comes from the regional register, not from a spreadsheet somebody last touched in March.

Below it, the divisions, ranked by execution. Mezam at ninety four per cent. Donga-Mantung at sixty five. A twenty nine point spread that no one in this House could see before, because nothing put the seven divisions side by side.

And here is the treasury position. Twenty point eight billion francs for the twenty twenty-six financial year. Eighteen point one billion in investment. Two point seven in operations.

This is the House record. What the Assembly has voted, accounted for, and decided on the floor, since it first sat in twenty twenty-one.

The budget, year by year. Three billion in twenty twenty-one. Twenty point eight billion today. A sevenfold rise in five years.

Look at what is missing. Three figures on this record have never been published. They are not hidden. They are shown, labelled, and counted, so the Assembly can see which of its own minutes are absent from its own record.

Every entry names the document it came from. A member can follow the citation during the sitting, and check it.

On the floor, every deliberation and resolution since twenty twenty-one. Seventy four entries, from the first budget vote to the customs clearing house.

Search it. The FEICOM financing chain. The dry port. NOWEDIF, from its creation in twenty twenty-one to the resolution that it must be funded properly. Threads that ran across five years of sittings, now visible in one place.

Minutes are drafted, laid before the House, and adopted, inside the platform. The record of a sitting stops being a file on somebody's laptop.

The registry holds the documents themselves. Fourteen filed, all searchable on their contents. Three of them were scans with no readable text at all. The platform read forty nine pages of them and recovered the figures inside.

That matters. Reading those scans is how we recovered the exact twenty twenty-two adjustment. Four billion, three hundred and forty eight million francs, where the press had only reported four point three billion.

Committee reports are filed here too. And where a report runs long, the platform will draft a summary of it on request, from the report's own text.

The programme register. Twelve programmes, one point one billion francs, across all seven divisions. Budget, contractor, division, and execution, for each one.

Execution is where the evidence lives. Each programme carries what the site reported, a photograph of the work, and what the community said about it. This is a stalled workshop, resumed after a three week stoppage. Six masons back on site.

The situation room shows what arrived today, and what did not. Three sites reported. Seven programmes filed nothing. Silence from a site is not the same as calm, so the platform names the silent ones.

One site is blocked. The science laboratory at Weh is structurally complete, waiting on benches from Douala. The building is finished, and locked.

None of this assumes a good connection. Collection holds for thirty days offline, and syncs the moment a network returns. In a region that has lost the internet for months at a time, that is not a convenience. It is the difference between a record and a gap.

Analytics turns the register into trend. Quarterly progress by sector. Budget allocation. Divisional comparison. The same figures, arranged for a decision.

Ask the Assembly answers questions from the register itself. Not from guesswork. If the register does not hold the answer, it says so.

Nine capacities, from Superadmin to Citizen. A field agent files reports. A clerk keeps minutes. A citizen may comment on a programme, and cannot see a minute that has not been laid before the House.

Because citizens are in this. Feedback on programmes, routed to the officer responsible. A culvert reported blocked in Kumbo. A fence that made a hospital compound secure at night.

And field officers can walk a site with a phone, and put it on this screen, live.

All of it runs on a Command Centre wall in the Assembly building. The live region, in the room where the decisions are taken.

And the case itself lives here. The slide deck put before the Council is a page in this platform, reading the same register you have just seen.
